How to Read The Machine

A plain-language guide to the selections, grades, and labels you see in every TrackSmart AI report — so you know exactly what The Machine is telling you before you make your play.

Start here. The Machine reads a race the way a seasoned handicapper reads it — asking how much early speed a horse has, how strong its late kick is, how it measures up in class, how fit and well-placed it is that day, and how sharp its connections are. It weighs all of that together and turns it into clear scores and rankings.

The difference is that it does this for every horse in every race the same way, every time — pouring through thousands of lines of code, rules, and handicapping angles, quickly and consistently, with no favorites and no gut feelings. Sometimes it has its opinion formed before the odds are even set, so what you're reading is a pure read on the horses, not a reaction to the crowd.

Below is what each term means. You don't need any of the math to use the reports — just this guide.

The Headline Numbers

Normalized TPN (0–100)

The single most important number in the report. It rolls everything The Machine knows about a horse into one score from 0 to 100, scaled to that specific race. The top horse in a race is anchored near 100, and everyone else is measured against it.

A big gap between the top horse and the next means The Machine sees a standout. A tight cluster of scores means a competitive, wide-open race.

Win Probability

The Machine's estimate of each horse's chance to win, shown as a percentage. It reflects both how good the horse is and how tough the rest of the field is.

AI Confidence Rank

How strongly The Machine feels about a race compared to the rest of the card, from #1 (most confident) down. A #1 rank means a clear, well-separated top pick; a high number means a chaotic or bunched race where anything can happen. Confidence is earned, so it's never spread evenly across the day.

Combined Win % (Top 3)

Add up the win chances of the top three selections and this is what you get. A high combined number means the race is likely to come from a small group; a lower number means the field is spread out and live for an upset.

The Letter Grades

A+ through D

Throughout the report, horses earn letter grades in different categories. The grades are always relative to the race in front of them — an "A" means top of this class, a "C" is par for the level, and a "D" is below it. It's a quick way to see a horse's strengths and weaknesses at a glance.

The Pace Picture

Projected Race Shape

The Machine's forecast for how the race will be run. Pace shapes it hands out include Loose on Lead (one horse controls an easy front), Alpha Controls (a dominant speed horse dictates terms), Contested / Honest (a fair, genuine pace), Tactical / Crawl (slow and patient), and Turf / Late-Kick Priority (a route where the finish decides it).

A projected shape is a forecast, not a guarantee. It can shift on things no handicapper controls — a bad or less-than-clean start, a rival breaking sharply and grabbing the front, a trainer changing tactics that day, or a jockey's split-second judgment as the race unfolds. But pace makes the race, and that's exactly why it's one of the most important parts of The Machine's analysis.

Early Speed Edge

Which horse (or horses) figures to be quickest to the front, and how strong that speed is. When one horse clearly owns the front, that's a major tactical advantage.

Late Kick Advantage

Which horse has the strongest finishing run, and who's best set up to use it. In races with a hot or collapsing pace, the late kick often wins the day.

The Pace Beneficiary

The horse the projected pace sets up perfectly. Two common versions:

Catbird / Garden Spot — the horse tracking just off the leader in the ideal stalking trip Chaos Survivor — the closer who benefits when a fast pace melts down up front

The Selections

The Setup & The Edge

For each top pick, The Setup is the story — the form, class move, or angle that makes the horse interesting today. The Edge is the hard reason it ranks where it does, tied back to its grades and Normalized TPN.

TrackSmart Alerts

Quick flags on a horse worth a second look. The ones you'll see most:

Live FTS — a first-time starter The Machine respects Live Overlay — priced higher than its true chance Lone Speed — likely uncontested on the lead Value Play — a longer-priced horse worth using Chaos Play — a live longshot if the race gets messy

Alternate Selection (Scratch Protection)

A backup pick. If any of the top four selections is scratched before post, this horse steps into the fourth slot so your play stays covered.

What Does The Machine Think?

One job, no bias

The Machine has a single mission: find the most likely winners, race after race, and do it the same disciplined way every time. It has no favorites, no loyalties, and no hunches — just a fresh, even-handed read on every horse in every field.

It keeps score on itself

The Machine doesn't just make picks — it grades its own results and tracks them over time. Across thousands of races, here's how its selections have held up:

~80%Winner in the Top 4
>50%Winner in the Top 2
~$8Avg. payout on hits

In plain terms: about four times out of five, the winner shows up somewhere in The Machine's top four selections — and more than half the time it's one of the top two.

It learns from what it misses

Just as important as the wins, The Machine studies the races it gets wrong. Every miss is reviewed and used to sharpen how it weighs pace, class, and form for next time. A good handicapper gets better with experience — The Machine does the same thing, only across far more races than any person could review by hand.

The bottom line: Start with the Normalized TPN to see who The Machine likes and by how much, check the Confidence Rank to see how sure it is about the race, and read the pace picture to understand why. The rest is detail that fills in the story. No handicapping tool wins them all — The Machine is built to give you a consistent edge and sharper insights, not a guarantee.
