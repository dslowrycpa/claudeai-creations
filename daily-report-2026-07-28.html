🔍 Race Notes: Behind the Numbers

The real value for members is understanding why a race played out the way it did. Here's what stood out today:

Scratch-fill protocol was the story of the day

Across three tracks the bump-up logic directly produced winners the model would otherwise have left blank: Justiceonemoretime (1st from 4th) and Coach Albert Lady (2nd from fringe) at Horseshoe Indianapolis, Cocktail Kisses (2nd from 3rd) at Presque Isle, and Maturation (1st from 2nd) at Finger Lakes.

Why It MattersWhen top selections scratch, the AI re-ranks the field rather than abandoning the race — so its Top 4 stays live on cards full of late defections, which is exactly when the public's sheets go stale.

Off-the-turf races remain the model's blind spot

The day's two ugliest misses shared a cause: surface change. Horseshoe Indianapolis Race 6 ($52.00) was scheduled for turf but run a mile on a muddy main track, and Finger Lakes ran its whole card over a Sloppy (Sealed) surface where both misses were off-track form reversals.

Why It MattersWhen a race is taken off the turf or the strip is sealed, recent form on the original surface is noisy — trust the model less in those specific spots until an off-the-turf override is in place.

Ground-saving stalkers keep beating raw speed figures

Presque Isle's fair All-Weather surface saw both AI misses (Spirited Sol, Nicole's Will) won by horses that saved ground on the rail and angled out late, and Fort Erie's Ya No did the same against a scratch-weakened field. Each was downgraded on raw speed numbers.

Why It MattersOn fair-to-tactical surfaces, trip and pace setup are outrunning raw speed figures right now — a signal to give tactical stalkers more credit than the figures alone suggest.

Data integrity, not handicapping, cost Race 1 at Fort Erie

Fort Erie Race 1 was won by the 1.55-1 favorite Truly Mischief, who was absent from the AI's analysis entirely due to a feed omission — the model handicapped a field missing its top contender.
