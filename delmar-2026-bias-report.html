Dirt: no real rail bias — turf: definite inside-to-mid bias

On the main track, the rail (post 1) has actually been the weakest inside post at 7.9%, while posts 2, 4 and 8 have all outperformed it. Dirt at Del Mar this meet is playing fair to slightly speed-favoring across a wide band of posts rather than punishing outside draws. Turf is a different story: post 4 (19.7% win, 48.5% ITM) and the rail (15.2% win, 43.9% ITM) have been the places to be, while posts 7 and out have combined for just 7 winners from 183 turf starts.

Turf routes reward patience, turf sprints reward the rail

Turf routes have been the most post-sensitive slice of the meet: post 4 alone has produced 56.1% ITM, while outside posts (8 and up) have barely factored. Turf sprints flip the early speed math — the rail has hit the board 60.0% of the time, the best ITM rate of any single post on this page. On the main track, dirt routes are the thinnest sample (only 19 races) and post 6's 25.0% is worth watching rather than betting on outright until the sample grows.

Press, don't just speed

Horses sitting just off the pace at the first call (running 2nd) have actually out-won the pure leaders this meet, 22.0% to 16.3%. Only 16.2% of winners meet-wide led wire-to-wire at the first call, and that number craters to 7.3% in turf routes — confirmation that the turf-route post bias toward the 4-hole is really a stalking-trip bias, not a speed bias. Dirt is the more speed-friendly surface: leaders and pressers combined account for a clear majority of dirt winners.

💡 Betting Angles

Respect the 4-hole, especially going long

Post 4 is best or co-best in the overall, dirt, turf and route breakdowns — the one exception is sprints, where post 5 takes over. When a legitimate contender draws post 4 in a route race, treat it as a real positive, not noise.

Turf outside posts are a real liability

Posts 7–10 on the turf course have combined for almost no wins this meet. Discount outside-post turf horses a notch below their morning-line price, especially in routes.

Don't assume the rail is dead on dirt

Unlike a lot of dirt strips, Del Mar's main track hasn't punished the rail this meet — it's just been unremarkable rather than golden. The bias on dirt is spread across posts 2 through 8, so use trip and pace, not post alone, to separate dirt contenders.

Turf sprints still favor speed and the rail together

The rail's strong ITM rate in turf sprints lines up with this being the one category where a clean speed trip from an inside post is genuinely powerful.

TrackSmart AI Read: Del Mar's dirt is playing close to fair with a mild lean toward posts 2–5, while the turf course is showing a real inside/mid bias that punishes wide draws — sharpest in routes, where stalking trips from the 4-hole have dominated. Track profiles shift as a meet wears in and the rail gets chewed up; this report will be worth refreshing again in a few weeks.
